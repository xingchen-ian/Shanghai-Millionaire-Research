Original finding

Color groups as western settlement boundaries

The dating clue is not price alone. It lies in how the board groups western roads: Great Western Road, Yu Yuen Road, and Connaught Road form a red cluster around the International Settlement's mature west-end life boundary, while the still farther-west Avenue Haig appears in the purple group rather than extending that red cluster.

Avenue Haig therefore strengthens the method: the board is not simply ranking streets by how far west they are. It seems to distinguish a recognizable concession-life edge from a more peripheral western road. This remains a working hypothesis to test against maps, advertisements, land values, and object evidence.

What it matches
  • The 1936 Waddington/London Monopoly context gives the Shanghai version a plausible lower bound.
  • 1933/1934 land-value maps are close enough to test the street hierarchy behind the board.
  • Late-1930s and early-1940s circulation clues fit a game designed around the war-break and isolated-settlement years.
How the date is inferred
  • The board uses a Monopoly-family form that likely postdates the 1935-1936 commercial spread of Monopoly.
  • Avenue Haig is farther west than the red group, yet it is not grouped with Great Western, Yu Yuen, and Connaught.
  • This suggests a social map of concession life and color grouping, not a simple westward-distance rule.
What still needs testing
  • Compare the red group with 1936-1941 street guides, real-estate ads, and newspaper descriptions.
  • Check whether maps and land-value bands place these roads at a recognizable west-end threshold.
  • Separate board design date, printing date, and later purchase or circulation date.
