Source Matrix

A compact evidence map: what each source proves, what it does not prove, and how it changes the research frame.

Evidence Matrix

Source Type Supports Limits Weight
Sihang collection copy Object evidence English commercial edition, Monopoly-family layout, Shanghai street localization, print quality. Does not identify printer, exact production date, or retail channel. High
World of Monopoly Collector documentation Unknown Shanghai maker, likely local manufacture, local-store circulation, Waddington influence. Does not name Wing On or provide primary retail records. Medium
Ralph Harpuder / Rickshaw.org Personal recollection Childhood play memory; recalls major department-store sales such as Wing On. Retail detail remains recollection, not a sales record. Medium-low
Powerhouse / Ross family Museum record Commercial copy purchased in Shanghai between August 1945 and November 1946; post-1940 circulation. Does not identify the shop or manufacturer. High
USHMM / Lobel brothers Museum object Handmade refugee-child version in 1946 Hongkew, made from K-ration cardboard. Proves copying and desire for play, not commercial distribution. High
Ernest Culman oral history Oral history Children played and copied Shanghai Millionaire from memory when commercial copies were unaffordable. Requires full transcript review for context and exact social setting. High
CSUN / Bell Family Collection Archive description Commercial game appears in an expatriate family collection tied to wartime Shanghai and Chapei internment context. Does not prove camp use or retail channel. Medium
JDC photograph and Hirsch story Photo / public-history record Game entered refugee children's activities and migration memory; game box could become a survival object. JDC day-camp activity is not the same as Japanese civilian internment camp use. Medium
Kimberly Cheng, "The Power of Play" Interpretive article Frames refugee children's play as a way of learning Shanghai's streets, social geography, and neighborhood hierarchy. Synthesizes sources; original testimonies and archival items still need separate checking. Medium-high
Joan Klyhn, "Shanghai Millionaire" Personal memory Adds another childhood-play memory and shows Shanghai Millionaire could be remembered before Monopoly. Blog memory; biographical and social context should be checked before heavy use. Medium
The Monopolist / Shanghai Real Estate Collector-market source Suggests a wider Shanghai ecosystem of Monopoly-like variants and possible manufacturing links. Does not prove wartime use, retail channel, or exact maker of Shanghai Millionaire. Low-medium
Use of this matrix: the project now separates object evidence, circulation evidence, play memory, and retail hypotheses. This prevents a recollection such as Wing On from being treated as the same kind of proof as a surviving object or museum acquisition record.

How the New Sources Change the Frame

From object study

The board is still the primary artifact, but it now sits inside a wider network of families, schools, refugee organizations, and collectors.

To social history

The strongest new pattern is not only who printed the game, but who played, copied, remembered, and carried it across migration.

To material culture

The game moved between entertainment, city knowledge, family memory, improvised craft, and even a container for hidden cash.

To evidence discipline

The site now marks retail claims, camp claims, and circulation claims separately instead of merging them into one narrative.
